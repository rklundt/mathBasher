# Sprint 2.12: Balance Scale — a ninth game mode

**Phase:** 2
**Status:** Planned — owner approved the art proofs and answered the questions (2026-10-25); design review next
**Closed:** —
**Released:** —

## Summary

The first game that isn't "pick the answer from a row": a cute balance scale at a sunny fair. One pan holds part of a number sentence with a missing number, the other the rest; the child drops the weight that makes both sides weigh the same. Wrong weights tip the scale the honest way ("Too heavy!" / "Too light!"), so the child learns which way they were off, and what "=" means. Owner: "start with balance scale, since that could fit all types" (2026-10-25).

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-25)

1. **Missing-number sentences, every challenge.** `12 + ___ = 17`, `___ + 12 = 17`, and reversed `17 = 12 + ___`, `17 = ___ + 12`; the same for subtract, multiply, divide, Mixed and fractions.
2. **Reversed forms only on Medium and Hard.** Easy uses the two `… = 17` forms.
3. **The scale starts off balance**; the right weight levels it; a wrong one over- or under-balances it by how far off it was. Movement quick but not snappy (a short swing and a wobble).
4. **A cheering crowd:** cute animals peek over the fences and cheer a right weight, look sad at a wrong one. **A variety of animals** so the scenes differ (a pool; a few per question).
5. **Six background versions** cycling between questions, kept to the sides so they never compete with the math.
6. **A winding path** on the right with 12 stepping stones that fill in as the round goes (a duckling hops along it); it replaces the HUD's progress dots and "Q: n/12" in this game.
7. **Math stays consistent with the other games:** 3 lives; a wrong weight costs a life and the child retries the same sentence; 12 questions; timer, speed bonus, Finish Bonus and Perfect Round as everywhere.
8. **Sounds:** existing ones to start; the owner may send a "clank" for a mismatched scale. **Music: the owner sent the background song (2026-10-25)**, its own loop.
9. **Art direction approved** from the proofs: bolder numbers on the weights, bigger animals (expressions must read on a phone).

## Design

### D1. The scene (1280 × 720)

- **Scale** in the middle: a pillar with a kawaii face (smiles when balanced, sad when off), a beam with an "=" badge at the pivot, two hanging pans. Each pan holds weights and an operator ("+", "−", "×", "÷"); the missing number is a dashed "?" slot on one pan.
- **Weight tray** along the bottom: 3 / 4 / 5 weights (Easy / Medium / Hard), kettlebell-shaped, pastel, big bold labels (`MathText`, so fractions and mixed numbers read clearly).
- **Tap a weight:** it flies onto the "?" slot; the beam swings to its new angle (≈ 400 ms, eased, small overshoot and wobble). Right: level, "Balanced!", crowd cheers, confetti. Wrong: tips toward the heavier side, "Too heavy!" / "Too light!", crowd sad, a life lost, the weight hops back to the tray, the sentence stays.
- **Tilt:** proportional to how far off the pans are, clamped (a big miss tips fully, a near miss only a little); the starting tilt shows the "?" side light.
- **Crowd:** 3 animals from a pool of about 8 (bear, kitten, piglet, bunny, panda, fox, lamb, mouse), changed between questions (never the same set twice running), each with idle / happy / sad poses. The duckling stays on the path.
- **Backgrounds:** a shared sunny-fair base (sky, hills, bunting) plus one of six side props (balloons, Ferris wheel, tents, windmill, kites, rainbow), cross-faded between questions, shuffled so all six show before repeating. Props only in the strips the pans never reach.
- **Path:** 12 stones up the right side, a flag at the top; green ✓ = right first try, amber = right after a miss; the current stone ringed; the duckling hops up a stone per right answer and cheers at the flag.
- **HUD:** the prompt line shows the same sentence (`12 + ? = 17`); the progress dots and "Q: n/12" are hidden for this game.

### D2. Missing-number questions (the main new piece)

Today a question is a finished prompt and its answer. Balance Scale needs the parts: left operand, operator, right operand, result (each with its value and display). Plan:
- Generators also return the sentence's parts (`Question.parts`, optional, all-or-none like the display fields), for every challenge, Mixed included.
- A pure, tested `toMissingNumber(question, form, count, rng)` turns them into a new question: the prompt in the chosen form, the missing number as the answer, and `count` sensible wrong weights near it (whole numbers: close values; fractions: neighbours with the same bottom; never negative, never a duplicate, always the right one included).
- Forms per difficulty (decision 2). Subtract and divide pick a blank that keeps numbers whole and in the challenge's range.
- Scoring unchanged: the challenge's multiplier applies as in every game.

### D3. Rules, timing and scoring

- Shared rules (`resolveRetryEvent`, `QuestionClock`), as Bunny Garden: a wrong pick costs a life and retries; out of lives ends the round; time out costs a life and retries.
- Speed bonus timed from when the weights can be tapped (`markOpen`), stopped at the tap (the swing isn't counted).
- Round end per ADR-0011 11c (`isRoundEnding`, `beginRoundEnd` at "Out of lives!").

### D4. Game Select and registration

- A ninth tile: Game Select becomes 3 × 3 (or 5 + 4; decided in the design review by what fits a phone).
- The new-game checklist (ADR-0011): `GameId`, scene key, `GAME_BG_MAP`, music and midground maps (borrowed until the owner sends a track), audio scopes, `gameSceneKeyFor`, Loading caption, Difficulty labels ("3 / 4 / 5 weights"), `questionsPerRoundFor`, config block, `MathText` labels, telemetry.

### D5. Art (drawn in code, like every game since 2.5.4)

`scripts/sprites/{scale,weights,fairCrowd,fair}.mjs`: the scale (pillar faces, beam, pans), weights (five pastel colours; labels drawn by the scene), the crowd sheets (8 animals × idle / happy / sad), the fair base and six side-prop layers, a darkened menu backdrop. WebP, budgets as before.
