## Summary

Wrap-up sprint for the three-game-mode era. After 2.2 shipped Number Climb at 12 floors (playtest finding: 10 felt short, 20 felt long, 12 is the sweet spot), the two arcade modes (Alien Shoot, Asteroid Field) are still at the global default of 20 questions per round. The asymmetry is a UX issue (one mode is meaningfully longer than the other two) AND a scoring issue (a 20-question round can score 1.67× the points of a 12-question round at the same difficulty, all else equal — distorts the cross-game high-score leaderboard once Phase 3 stores them).

Story 1 brings the arcade modes down to 12 to match Climb. Story 2 walks the scoring math across all three games at every Easy/Medium/Hard combination and confirms the THEORETICAL MAX score for a perfect round is identical across games at each difficulty — so a kid's "best score on Easy" is comparable regardless of which game they played.

The sprint is intentionally open-ended past these two stories so polish items that emerge during cross-game playtest can land in the same sprint instead of fragmenting into one-shot commits.

## Stories

### Story 1: Arcade modes drop from 20 → 12 questions per round

**Type:** Code (config + ripple verification)

**Context:** Sprint 2.2 closed with Number Climb at 12 floors after playtest feedback ("20 is too long, 10 is too short, 12 is the sweet spot"). Alien Shoot + Asteroid Field still use the global `config.round.questionsPerRound = 20`. Asymmetry between modes makes the three-mode lineup feel inconsistent + skews max scores per Story 2.

**Output:**
- `config.round.questionsPerRound: 20 → 12`.
- Since Number Climb passes `12` explicitly to its `RoundController` constructor as an override (story 10 in sprint 2.2), the override now equals the default. Two paths:
  - **Option A** (minimal change): leave Climb's explicit override in place — explicit is fine even when it matches the default.
  - **Option B** (cleanup): remove the override since it's now redundant.
  - **Recommendation: Option A.** Keeps Climb's "round size is mine, not the global default" signal intact in code, which matters if the global default ever shifts again.
- Audit `config.round.starThresholds` — currently calibrated for a 20-question round (likely `[6, 12, 18]` or similar, i.e. 30 % / 60 % / 90 % of 20). Re-scale to 12: `[4, 8, 11]` (33 % / 67 % / 92 %) OR keep the percentage ladder via a helper similar to `computeClimbStars`. Pick whichever is less invasive.
- Verify HUD progress dots fit gracefully at 12 (already proven for Climb — the dots layout is data-driven from `roundController.questionsPerRound`).
- Verify GameOver "Correct: N / 12" — already auto-reads from the same source (sprint 2.2 stories 15 / 15a).
- Manual playtest both arcade modes start-to-end on Easy + Hard to confirm the shorter round still feels like a complete game session.

**Acceptance:**
- Alien Shoot + Asteroid Field rounds end after 12 questions.
- Star thresholds adjust so a strong-but-imperfect round still earns 3 stars at the right cadence (the prior 20-question ladder demanded ~18 correct for 3★; on 12, the equivalent feel is ~11 correct).
- Number Climb remains at 12 (no regression).
- All 337 tests stay green; star-threshold tests in `ScoreCalculator.test.ts` may need their hardcoded numbers updated if they were written against the 20-question ladder.

### Story 2: Cross-game max-score calibration (Easy / Medium / Hard)

**Type:** Code (audit + adjust scoring multipliers)

**Context:** With Story 1 normalizing all three games to 12 questions, the theoretical max score per game at each difficulty SHOULD be identical — but only if the scoring formula's inputs match across modes. The current `ScoreCalculator.recordOutcome` computes `basePerCorrect × mathDifficulty × speedMultiplier × wrongShotMultiplier` and is GLOBAL — same formula for all three games. So in theory max scores match. But: there are mode-specific scoring nuances that may drift the max:

- **Alien Shoot:** the descending alien penalty (sprint 0.5 wrong-shot mechanic) reduces score over the round.
- **Asteroid Field:** per-question countdown — running out of time scores 0 for that question.
- **Number Climb:** one mulligan per floor (sprint 2.2) — first wrong rung halves points for that floor; the cumulative timer running out ends the round early.

These create different SCORE-CEILING RISKS per game even though the BASE formulas are identical. A kid playing perfectly on Easy should walk away with the same max score regardless of which game they picked. If not, the cross-game leaderboard (Phase 3) becomes "which game is most generous" rather than "who's best at math."

**Output:**
- Compute the THEORETICAL MAX score for each (game × difficulty) combo by walking the scoring formula assuming a perfect round (no wrong shots / no mulligan / no timeouts):
  - 12 correct × basePerCorrect × mathMultiplier × speedMultiplier
  - With `mathMultiplier` collapsed to its highest value (add-to-10 = 1.0 is the floor; div-to-144 = 4.0 is the ceiling — pick a fixed reference like `add-to-10` for the cross-game comparison since math choice is orthogonal to game choice).
- Document the resulting max-score grid in a table (3 games × 3 difficulties = 9 numbers).
- If any cell differs from another at the same difficulty: identify the game-specific multiplier that's diverging, decide whether to converge it (preferred) or document the intentional divergence.
- Likely candidate for divergence: each game's `config.<gameId>.speed` block may have its own implicit difficulty factor (Climb's cumulative timer makes Hard much harder than Asteroid Field's per-question 12s, even if the SPEED multiplier in scoring is the same). May need a small per-game "difficulty-adjustment multiplier" added so a perfect Climb-Hard round and a perfect AsteroidField-Hard round produce the same max score.
- Update `ScoreCalculator.test.ts` to ASSERT the cross-game parity at each difficulty as a regression guard.

**Acceptance:**
- A perfect Easy round of any of the three games produces the same final score.
- Same for Medium. Same for Hard.
- The math-difficulty multiplier still varies (add-to-10 vs div-to-144 still score differently); the game-mode axis is what gets normalized.
- New tests in `ScoreCalculator.test.ts` lock the parity so a future tuning change can't silently desync the games again.
